A paper poster on the wall tells you to take Staircase 2. But what if Staircase 2 is filled with dense black smoke? The paper sign leads people straight into the hazard.

How SafetyBand Works

Turning physical buildings into mathematical networks that can be solved instantly.

CONCEPT • GRAPH THEORY MADE SIMPLE

The Three Building Blocks

1. Rooms are "Nodes": Classrooms, labs, stairways, and exits are numbered dots.

2. Hallways are "Edges": The walking paths connecting rooms, each with a physical distance score.

3. Hazards are "Weight Multipliers": If a hall is crowded, its cost triples ($3\times$). If it's on fire, its cost becomes infinity ($\infty$).

"A path with fire is mathematically treated as an impassable brick wall."

The Brain: Dijkstra's Algorithm

A proven, bulletproof algorithm used by internet routers and global logistics.

THE ALGORITHM • IN SIMPLE WORDS

How Dijkstra Finds the Route

Imagine water flowing from your room through every open door in the building. Water naturally takes the path of least resistance and reaches an exit first along the easiest route.

Dijkstra’s Algorithm works the exact same way mathematically: it searches every branch, adds up walking distance + hazard penalties, and returns the cleanest escape corridor.

Time complexity: O((V + E) log V) with a Min-Heap. Runs in under a millisecond.
HAZARD SCALING • THE FORMULA

Penalty Table

Normal Hallway Cost × 1 (Clear Walk)
Crowded Concourse Cost × 3 (Slow Moving)
Smoke / Bottleneck Cost × 5 (High Risk)
Active Fire / Collapse Cost = ∞ (COMPLETELY SEALED)

The algorithm never routes a human being into a room with infinite cost.
